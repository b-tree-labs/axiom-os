# ADR-250: A run is the unit of isolation, a chain composes runs, a grant is one noun, and the node writes the record

**Status:** Proposed (2026-11-10)
**Related:** epic #1722; the review `docs/working/unified-agent-workspaces-review-2026-11-10.md` (draft PR #1725); [prd-runs-workspaces-and-support](../prds/prd-runs-workspaces-and-support.md) (the product design); [ADR-183](adr-183-a-site-is-maintained-by-signed-requests-it-fetches-and-sessions-it-opens.md) (remote support, which this extends); [ADR-231](adr-231-a-chain-of-hand-offs-has-executor-semantics.md) and [ADR-237](adr-237-every-agent-runs-on-one-harness-an-agent-is-a-contract.md) (chains and the baton); [ADR-233](adr-233-chat-spawns-subagents-through-one-manager-and-one-run-handle.md) (the run handle and the manager); [ADR-236](adr-236-one-permission-model-deny-ask-notify-allow.md) (one permission model); [ADR-142](adr-142-attestation-is-the-record-of-accountable-human-acts.md) and [ADR-146](adr-146-signing-from-the-browser.md) (signing); [ADR-163](adr-163-steer-and-log-are-two-projections-of-one-journal.md) and [ADR-241](adr-241-a-record-lives-with-its-writer-gold-serves-what-is-derived.md) (the journal and gold); [ADR-205](adr-205-a-key-belongs-to-a-person-and-what-it-reads-is-resolved-from-grants-and-sharing-programs.md) (read grants); [ADR-209](adr-209-every-installation-is-a-site.md) (a workspace is never a site); `spec-aeos-0.1.md` §4.5, §4.7, §4.9, §4.13, §6.4.

## Context

Epic #1722 designs three things as one body of work: agents delegating coding work to harnesses in isolated containers, runs that compose into chains and standing agents on events, and remote support that is brokered, consented and levelled. An adversarial review of its six parts (PR #1725) found that most of the primitives exist and are strong: ADR-183's signed requests and sessions over an outbound relay, GUARD's `decide()`, `CapabilityToken` with a classification ceiling, a validity window, delegation depth, a parent and a budget envelope, `gate invite`, the hash-chained audit trail, the attest books with `delegated` and `revoked`, the lane registry, the process registry, the memory serving gate, the cross-mem absorb adapters, the forge webhook receiver and PULSE. It also found five faults that are cheaper to fix before code than after: Part 3 rebuilt ADR-183 beside itself; the consent as written could be issued by an agent in the developer's own session; the record was to be written inside the untrusted workspace with a key the sandbox was meant to deny; about half the "already in Axiom" table was staging; and the vocabulary added some twenty nouns, several colliding with words the tree already uses (`task`, `lane`, `grant`, `session`, `level`, `ledger`).

The owner decided six things on 2026-11-10: support extends ADR-183; the unit of work and isolation is a run; the development lane is renamed workspace; one `grant` noun with a `kind`; the node writes the record and the harness transcript is evidence; a level-2 patch arrives only as a branch and a pull request. Five more are proposed here and await the owner.

## Decision

**A run is the unit of isolation, a chain is the unit of composition, a workspace is where a run executes, a grant is the one way authority is given, and the node writes the record.** Seven nouns (agent, skill, run, chain, workspace, grant, journal) and ten verbs name the whole model; everything else is a skill. Remote support is ADR-183 with amendments, never a second system.

### D1. A run is the unit of work and of isolation, and its placement is a field on the run

A run has a handle, a budget, a placement and a result (`RunHandle`, `RunManager`, `run_` ids, ADR-233). The word `task` is not used: it already names `axi tasks`. Placement is an `executor` field with four values, `host`, `workspace`, `node`, `remote`; `binding` (`local`, `remote`) stays the transport axis. A caller may narrow placement and never widen it, as with budget. `delegate` is `spawn_agent` with `isolation: workspace`: the container form of the subagent ADR-233 §8 already defines, dispatched through the one chokepoint, with `RunManager` gaining its first production caller. A `container` executor starts the harness with `docker exec` against a claimed workspace; the local contract is `docker exec` plus the run handle's poll tools, and no A2A endpoint runs inside a container. A run above the executor's classification ceiling is refused at dispatch with the reason, before anything is built.

### D2. A chain composes runs; edges carry artifacts and never state; the baton attenuates

A chain is a graph of runs and the graph is data (ADR-231, ADR-237 decision 2), so it is journaled, inspectable and resumable (a completed run with the same inputs is reused). Edges carry typed artifacts (a branch at a commit, a pull request, a bundle, a gold object reference, a brief, a decision), never a filesystem, a session, a cookie or a credential. The baton carries deadline, authority and budget downward only: `Baton.child()` receives at most a subset of the parent's grant and at most a slice of its budget, and the dispatcher refuses a child that would widen either. Sharing (a shared workspace for a declared sequence, a read-only cache layer, a memory grant) is a declared edge GUARD can refuse. Dynamic spawn is bounded by permission rules for depth and fan-out and by one chain budget that depth, fan-out and retries all draw from. Fan-in waits on a verifier's evidence with provenance on the envelope (run, grant, verifier), never on the child's word and never on time; a rule may require a person's signature before a wider grant acts on a narrower run's artifact. A person's step references an attestation in its logbook; the chain never holds the signature. A standing agent's chain names its accountable person from `permissions.toml` or the repository's `.axiom/permissions.toml`; without one it does not fire.

### D3. A workspace is where a run executes; the development lane is renamed workspace, with no alias

The lane registry (name, root, branch, trees, venvs, ports, database, owner, holds, claim time) is the workspace registry under its right name: `axi workspace` with the same verbs (`claim`, `list`, `release`, `hold`, `drop`, `doctor`), skills `workspace.*`, no alias, and the registry gains `executor` and `classification_ceiling`. The journal keeps the word lane (ADR-163). A workspace is claimed with an owner and an expiry through the one governed path (ADR-237 decision 9), is bound to its owner's node (ADR-195) and is never a site: `axi setup` inside one is refused, it holds no node key and no site record. A workspace runs one run at a time: a headless harness, the repository at a branch, tools, read-only caches, and a grant that expires with the run; no vault, no signing surface. A declared workspace is a `[workspace]` root section in the manifest (D7). Remote and hosted executors default to a ceiling of `internal` (P3, proposed).

### D4. One grant noun, four kinds, one shape, issued by KEEP

Authority is given one way: a grant. The existing three become kinds under one noun, with code names unchanged and internals merged later: `read` (ADR-205 role grants, what a key may read), `act` (capability tokens in `governance/capability.py`, what an actor may do), `sign` (ADR-146 signing grants in `attest/grants.py`, sign once), and `run` (a capability token with a budget, a parent and an expiry equal to the run's, minted by `delegate`). Every kind projects one shape: principal, scope, expiry, issuer, parent. KEEP issues all four and owns every expiry (ADR-209 D8a); one list (`axi grant list`) and one revoke (`axi grant revoke`) cover them all. A grant is Axiom's assertion of authority, checked by Axiom. A forge credential is a different thing: a per-run installation token from a forge App held by KEEP on the node, served to a credential helper inside the workspace through a socket on the host, which signs or refuses each request by the run's grant. No forge token is placed in a workspace, and branch protection on the default branch is a precondition RIVET checks before any workspace is granted push.

### D5. The node writes the record, before and after each step; the transcript is evidence

The node's dispatcher writes the record for every step of a run, before the body runs and after it returns, as the audit trail already does for skill calls (a keyed hash chain, `prev_hash`, chain ids). What crosses the workspace boundary through Axiom is recorded there: skill calls through the chokepoint, tool calls through the MCP server, subprocesses through the process registry, each with the run id and chain id. A run executing in a workspace is recorded by the node that dispatched it, never by the workspace; the workspace holds no key. Arguments are stored under field-level redaction rather than a digest: a capability declares `redact = ("param", ...)` and the named values are replaced before the record is written. The harness's own transcript is absorbed afterwards through the cross-mem adapters (`memory/absorb/sessions.py`, `sessions_files.py`), linked to the run and labelled `self-reported`; it is evidence, never the record. The completeness claim is "every action that passed through Axiom", tested with a negative control. Runs, grants and support events are lanes of the journal, projected to gold like every lane; there is no workspace ledger, no org ledger and no copy in between (ADR-241 D6 is unchanged).

### D6. Remote support is ADR-183, amended: consent levels, a container-backed level 3, the journal roll-up

Support for a developer's own node is ADR-183 end to end: `maintenance = off | requests | sessions` declared at the machine's own command line, signed requests from a code allow-list fetched over the node's outbound connection, the node as the authority, sessions a person at the site opens, the existing relay on the ADR-177 edge or a node the developer's node is bound to. Three amendments, each to land in its own PR against ADR-183:

- **Consent levels.** Four levels the developer consents to, mapped onto the declaration and the allow-list: level 0 (look) is the `support_bundle` and `diagnose` read actions; level 1 (diagnose) adds three read-only actions with parameter schemas, `versions`, `ports`, `db_ping`, never repository code; level 2 (propose) adds one `propose` action of risk `change` whose payload is a branch at a commit, a pull request reference and a description, shown in `axi maintenance pending` with the diff and the risk per step, approved into a pull request through the forge and never applied on the host (owner decision 6); level 3 (hands on) is a `sessions` session. The consent is a human-only ceremony: `axi support request` is CLI-only, never projected to MCP or an agent tool, exactly as `features maintenance` is; it opens the ADR-146 ceremony and writes a `delegated` entry in an `access` logbook declared by the `maintenance` extension (supporter handle, level, expiry, workspace); `revoked`, `support close` and `features maintenance off` end it. The supporter resolves to a known principal (ADR-103 directory or a declared federation peer) and is a distinct actor on every request, never the node's own principal. KEEP mints the supporter an invitation bound to the consent (`gate invite`); HERALD delivers it.
- **A container-backed level 3.** One wiring line: `support_shell = docker exec -i <workspace> bash` against a workspace the developer has claimed. `support open` is refused when no workspace is claimed. The session is recorded on both ends as today.
- **The journal roll-up.** The node's audit decisions and the relay's results are referenced from the journal as support events so the Timeline shows a session whole. Attachments (the support bundle, a diagnosis) are immutable content-addressed blobs in the node's object store, referenced by hash inside the signed entry, served through the attest door with the entry's labels, and indexed through the Library's extraction path as a labelled derived index when the type warrants.

Nothing a supporter sends executes on the developer's host: level 1 is read-only, level 2 lands on the forge, level 3 runs in a workspace. Level 0 output is routed through the support-bundle rule (allow-listed fields, `secret_scan`, a bounded window), never raw files. Vault contents, ungranted memory, controlled code above the executor's ceiling and the person's own browser never cross.

### D7. The AEOS delta, as the review accepted it

`spec-aeos-0.1.md` is not edited by the PR that lands this ADR; it is amended in its own PR (build plan unit RW-A1). The core kind count stays at eight. The delta:

- **§6.4, a `[workspace]` root section** (strict): `from_setup` (the product's native setup script, so two paths cannot drift), `services`, `caches`, `egress` (the `x-axiom-egress` shape), `limits` (`cpus`, `memory_gb`, `disk_gb`, `wall_clock_s`), `classification_ceiling`, `harnesses`. Lint: an `egress` entry without `purpose` is an error; `classification_ceiling` above `internal` on a workspace whose `executor` may be `remote` is an error.
- **§4.13, an `executor` field** with values `host`, `workspace`, `node`, `remote`; `binding` stays the transport. Lint: `executor = "workspace"` requires a reachable `[workspace]` declaration (AEOS167); `executor = "remote"` keeps AEOS166 (a verifier is required); a run whose inputs carry a classification above the executor's ceiling is refused at dispatch, not at lint.
- **§4.5, the harness adapter contract** under the existing adapter kind: `kind = "adapter"`, `integration = "harness"`, `capabilities = ["run_headless", "transcript", "completion"]`, providing `start(workspace, brief, budget) -> handle`, `transcript(handle) -> path`, `finished(handle) -> outcome`, and naming the absorb adapter that reads its sessions. No `harness` kind.
- **§4.7, two normative sentences on event payloads:** an event that starts a chain carries `trusted` (verified sender, repository, event id, received time) apart from `untrusted` (the body), and a runtime passes the body to any model in the data channel of ADR-232 part 5, never as instructions; every inbound capability URL is a KEEP-issued secret with an expiry and a revocation, never a bare URL.
- **§4.9.3, one runtime rule, and §4.9.7, one field:** the dispatch chokepoint writes the audit record before the body runs, and a run executing in a workspace is recorded by the node that dispatched it; `redact = ("param", ...)` names the inputs whose values are replaced before arguments are recorded. Lint at level B: a capability with `risk` above `read` and an input named like a secret declares `redact`.

Rejected: a `harness`, `trigger`, `delegation`, `ledger`, `task`, `bot` or `standing agent` kind, and a `broker` service. A consent needs no AEOS change: it is a logbook declaration (ADR-142 `delegated` and `revoked`, ADR-163 `kind = "logbook"`).

### Proposed, owner decision pending

Each is written as the review recommended and is marked pending until the owner decides.

- **P1 (review decision 7). The harness's model access** is a KEEP-issued per-run credential through Axiom's LLM gateway, metered against the run's budget envelope; a workspace never receives a vendor key. Until decided, budgets drawn by a harness are labelled advisory on the receipt.
- **P2 (review decision 8). Build order:** support levels 0 to 2 and `delegate` over `docker exec` before any container tooling, against devcontainers developers already make by hand.
- **P3 (review decision 9). A classification ceiling on workspaces**, checked at placement by GUARD (a workspace is not a node, so this is not WARDEN's); hosted and remote executors default to `internal`; the image builder refuses a package whose declared classification exceeds the ceiling. Until decided, remote executors are not offered.
- **P4 (review decision 10). `MAX_DELEGATION_DEPTH` rises from 2 to 4** only under a per-chain dollar budget enforced where money leaves (P1), with depth and fan-out caps as permission rules.
- **P5 (review decision 11). Axiom owns the generic `.devcontainer` template** (`axi dev container init`), written from the `[workspace]` declaration and built from the product's setup script; a product extends it; no product or site identifier in the template.

## Options considered

- **Build Part 3 beside ADR-183** (a broker, `support.*` tools, a support inbox, levels 0 to 3 as a new ladder). Lost: two support systems, two level ladders, two relays, two reviews by an information security office, and a second answer to "who is the authority". ADR-183 is built and proven on a replica of a production edge.
- **`task` as the unit of isolation.** Lost: it collides with `axi tasks` and ADR-232's task handle, and the thing with a handle, a budget, a placement and a state already exists as a run.
- **Keep both `lane` and `workspace`.** Lost: two words for one registry, and `lane` meaning two things inside Axiom. The journal needs the word; the registry does not.
- **Three grant vocabularies** (role grant, capability token, signing grant). Lost: three names in documents and screens for one idea. Code names stay; the noun and the shape unify.
- **A write-ahead ledger inside the workspace, signed with a workspace key.** Lost: the key is what an attacker wants, "read-only to the harness" is not a boundary inside one container, and a third-party harness's model calls never pass through it anyway. The party being recorded cannot keep the record.
- **Apply a level-2 patch on the developer's host on a yes.** Lost: code execution on a laptop behind a trusted-looking diff. The forge gives review and CI for free.
- **An A2A endpoint inside the container on a forwarded port.** Lost: a non-node speaking A2A to its own host, against ADR-232 part 7 and ADR-237 decision 11; `docker exec` and the run handle are the whole local contract.

## Consequences

- Easier: one model a newcomer learns from one paragraph; one place authority is given, listed and revoked; one record with one reader; support that an information security office has already reviewed once; a sandbox on macOS and Windows by way of the container, which ADR-036 D10 could not give.
- Harder: a rename with no alias touches the `lane` extension, its skills, its tests, the post-merge hook and about twenty documents in one PR; the `redact` field is new work in the chokepoint and a lint; the forge credential helper and the per-run forge identity are new code and pay the "no forge App identity" gap ADR-237 names.
- Committed to: the harness transcript is never promoted to the record; a workspace never holds a key; a supporter never executes on a host; a brief is a typed artifact; budgets attenuate and are enforced where money leaves once P1 is decided; a standing agent without an accountable person does not fire.
- Honest limits: Axiom records what passed through Axiom. A harness's vendor calls are visible only in its self-reported transcript until P1 routes them through the gateway. KEEL is blind on macOS, so the workspace lease and deadline must not depend on vitals.
- Follow-up: the build plan `docs/working/runs-workspaces-and-support-plan.md` (three waves, eighteen units, each a child issue of #1722); the ADR-183 amendment PR; the AEOS amendment PR; the owner's decisions P1 to P5.

## Amends

Nothing is edited in this PR; each amendment is named here and lands in its own PR.

- **ADR-183.** Adds the consent levels (four levels mapped onto `off | requests | sessions` and the allow-list), three read-only actions (`versions`, `ports`, `db_ping`) and one `propose` action, the CLI-only consent ceremony writing a `delegated` entry in an `access` logbook, the supporter as a distinct actor bound to a consent, a container-backed level 3 (`support_shell` wired to a claimed workspace; refused without one), the level-0 route through the support-bundle rule, and the journal roll-up with content-addressed attachments.
- **ADR-241.** D6's journal row is unchanged. This ADR adds runs, grants and support events as lanes of the journal, each projected to gold like the others, with no intermediate copy.
- **ADR-237.** Chains: a child run's grant and budget are subsets of its parent's, carried by the baton; sharing is a declared edge; dynamic spawn is appended to the same chain record and bounded by one chain budget; fan-in waits on evidence with provenance on the envelope; `delegate` is `spawn_agent` with `isolation: workspace`, the container form of decision 7's worker.
